How to use it

Where to run it: Claude Cowork on top of your business brain is best. Next best is Cowork in a workspace or project.

  1. Copy the Mirror below.
  2. Open Claude. Pick Sonnet 5.5 with thinking on high.
  3. Check each connector uses your work account. Your work Slack and work email, not a personal inbox or a community Slack.
  4. Send it, then pick your priorities when it asks.

In Claude chat, turn on chat search first. It’s built to take about 5 minutes, and it asks you one question about your priorities before it runs.

Prompt 1

The Mirror

Looks back at everything you made with AI in the last 30 days and shows you how much of it went anywhere, where your time went, and what to stop.

What you get

  • Where your time went, measured against the priorities you pick
  • The exact projects and topics to stop, pause or archive, by name and date
  • A share card with your numbers for the room

About 5 minuteson Sonnet 5.5.

THE MIRROR

Give me a five-minute audit of everything I've made with AI in the last 30 days: how much of it went anywhere, and what I keep making that goes nowhere. Skip any plan and keep it short.

Start by finding what looks like my top priorities right now from whatever you can see quickly: our recent chats and projects, any instructions or notes you have for me, and the titles of my recent meetings if you can see them. Keep it to a handful of searches. Then ask me one question with the AskUserQuestion tool, or in one short message if you don't have it: offer up to four priorities you found, each with a short line on where you saw it, and let me pick one or more or write my own. If you can't find enough to suggest, just ask what matters most. After that, don't ask me anything else until you're done.

Look only at what you can see quickly: our past chats, projects and artifacts, any files you can reach, and anything published. Work from names, dates and when things were last changed. Don't open documents, transcripts or code.

For proof that something went somewhere, use the quickest sign you have: it was changed or reused after the day it was made, it was shared or published, or it comes up by name in my team's chat or meeting notes. Keep it to about 15 searches, and spend them on the biggest items. If you search chat or email, use my team's workspace and skip any community Slack or personal inbox.

Write the result right here in the chat as clean markdown that's easy to scan: short lines, bold for the numbers that matter, no walls of text.

Open with one statement, built from my own numbers, that makes me stop and think hard about where my priorities went. Make it true, and make it land.

## Your priorities
The priorities I picked, one line each. If the pile points to a different priority than the ones I picked, say so here.

## Where your time went
What I put into AI this month: how many things I made, how many went somewhere, how many served my priorities, and which types I made most. Add one line on what the things that went somewhere have in common. Then the three things I put the most time into that served none of my priorities, by name and date, with what you judged the time from (versions, chats about it, days between first and last change). Use a small table where it helps.

## What to deprioritize
A short list of the exact things to stop, pause or archive, each by its real title and date, with one line on why. Group repeats under their topic, like "Brand style labs (5 versions, Nov 3 to Nov 9)," so nothing hides in a count. Lead with the topic I keep coming back to that goes nowhere.

Then the share card below, filled in with numbers only and no notes inside it. Close with one short line on what you counted from.

Hold to these:
- Be ruthless. Name what went nowhere, with dates, and don't soften it to spare my feelings.
- Every number is something you counted.
- "Went nowhere" only means you found no sign. You can't see who read what.
- If you can't see something, say what's missing in one line and keep going.

Instructions

The 5 secrets

One rule you paste into your Claude instructions so it asks before it builds anything.

What you get

  • Claude asks what pain it solves and which of your top goals it moves before it builds anything
  • A one-page plan you OK before anything gets built
  • A walk through every section before your team treats it as truth

About 2 minutesPaste it into your CLAUDE.md, project instructions or Cowork.

Before you build anything for me or my team (an app, dashboard, SOP, playbook, skill, artifact or new offer):
- Ask what pain this is causing, what it costs my business, and which of my top goals fixing it would move. If it moves none, say so before we build.
- Interview me one question at a time and write a one-page plan. Open it with one line on the pain it solves, then the must-haves, what’s out of scope, and what done looks like. Wait for my OK.
- Ask where the truth for this lives. It can’t live in what you build.
- Show me how the best do it, give me a few versions, and build the simplest.
- Walk me through every section before it’s saved where my team treats it as truth.
If this will take more than an hour of my time, ask whether it’s the best use of that hour this week.
Quick lines

The 5 lines to tell Claude

One line for each secret. Say it when you start a build, or keep all 5 in your instructions.

What you get

  • One line each for Earn, Plan, Ground, Borrow and Perfect
  • Copy one line, or all 5 at once
  • Short enough to say to Claude at the start of any build

Under a minuteUse them every time you build.

  1. 1 · Earn

    Before you build anything, ask me what problem we’re solving and whether it’s necessary. If I don’t have a reason, don’t build it.

  2. 2 · Plan

    Interview me first. Write a one-page brief and wait for my OK before you build.

  3. 3 · Ground

    Ask me where the truth for this lives. It can’t live in the artifact. Flag anything that makes a second copy.

  4. 4 · Borrow

    Research how the best show this, give me 5 versions to react to, then build the simplest one a person can take in.

  5. 5 · Perfect

    Walk me through every section and make me justify it. Then ask where it gets saved: scratch, mine or the department. The department needs an approval.
