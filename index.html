Just Because AI Can Doesn’t Mean You Should

These are the prompts from our session. Tap one to open it, hit Copy, and paste it into Claude.

Callan Faulkner, Kinsey Machos and Carter Jensen

How to use it

Where to run it: Claude Cowork on top of your business brain is best. Next best is Cowork in a workspace or project.

  1. Copy the Mirror below.
  2. Open Claude. Pick Sonnet 5.5 with thinking on high.
  3. Check each connector uses your work account. Your work Slack and work email, not a personal inbox or a community Slack.
  4. Send it, then answer its first few questions.

In Claude chat, turn on chat search first. It takes about 20 minutes, so start it early and let it run. If it asks you a question, answer it and let it keep going.

Prompt 1

The Mirror

Looks back at everything you made with AI in the last 30 days, checks whether each thing got used, and gives you a plan for the next 7 days.

What you get

  • Your funnel: what you made, what went somewhere and what moved something forward
  • Your top random acts of AI, with dates and proof
  • A plan for your next 7 days, with drafts of any messages you need to send

About 20 minuteson Sonnet 5.5, so start it early.

THE MIRROR

I've been building with AI for months: artifacts, docs, SOPs, playbooks, dashboards, skills, plans, new ideas. It feels productive. I'm not sure it is. I can't tell, because I made all of it. You can see the whole pile at once.

Work through this one step at a time, and judge every item on its own, like a grain of rice. Be thorough in how you judge and light in how you read: names, dates and search snippets are enough. Never open a whole transcript or document to look for a mention.

Step 1. Find what matters most to me right now. Take a quick look at where my priorities are written down: goals, quarterly rocks, plans, the main files of my business brain, and the summaries of my recent meetings. Skim titles and summaries only, and keep it to a handful of searches. Pick out up to four things that look like they matter most right now, with one short line each on what you saw.

Step 2. Ask me everything at once. Use the AskUserQuestion tool if you have it. If not, ask in one short message.
- What matters most to me right now? Offer what you found, let me pick one or more, and let me write my own. If you couldn't find enough to suggest, just ask.
- Where do I make things with AI? Offer what you can see connected, plus places people often build that you might not see: other AI chats like ChatGPT or Gemini, app builders like Replit, Lovable or Bolt, and design tools like Canva or Gamma.
- Where does my team talk about work (chat, email, meetings)?
- Where does finished work live or go out to people?
Search only the places I pick. Skip every other connected tool, and always skip a personal inbox, a community Slack or a client's workspace. Check that each place I pick is connected to my work account or workspace, and tell me if it isn't.
If I pick a place you can't see, don't stop. Give me two choices: paste a quick list of what I made there in the last 30 days (names are enough), or skip it for now. If I skip it, keep going without it. Never ask me to connect or install anything during this run, and never ask about the same place twice. Note every place you couldn't check.

Step 3. Build the inventory. List everything I've made with AI in the last 30 days in the places I picked, plus anything I pasted. Work from names, titles and dates, and don't open each one. Leave nothing out. Count one thing per purpose: drafts and versions of the same thing count once, and note how many versions there were. For each one, note what it is, the type and the date it was made. Show me the count and your counting rule before you go on.

Step 4. Look for proof that each item came up again: a mention, a reply, an edit, a link, a decision. For meetings, use titles, summaries and notes, never full transcripts. For apps and sites, use the app list, whether it was published and when it was last changed, and don't read the code. Where you can, pull one list per place for the last 30 days (meeting titles and summaries, recently edited pages) and check every item against it instead of searching one by one. Where you have to search, use the item's name plus one other word for it, and judge from the snippets. Stop at the first clear sign. Write down what you found, with the source and date, or "nothing found."

Step 5. Give each item one verdict: never went anywhere, shared then silence, made twice, became truth without review, came up again, or moved something forward. If something is too new to judge, say so.

Step 6. Check the ones I'll see by name. Before you list anything as a top random act of AI, search for it once more with different words. Change the verdict if you find something.

Step 7. Find the pattern. Compare the items that moved something forward with the rest. What do the wins have in common? What do the misses have in common? How much of the pile served what matters most to me?

Step 8. If something would change the plan and you can't work it out yourself, ask me up to three questions with the AskUserQuestion tool.

Step 9. Write the Mirror: the funnel (made, went somewhere, came up again, moved something forward), my random acts of AI with dates and proof, the duplicates, the pattern, and what I built instead of what matters most.

Step 10. Write my next 7 days: up to three focuses with reasons, what I'm not doing this week from my own pile, what to archive or merge, where the truth lives for anything with copies, a check I can run again in 7 days, and drafts of any messages I need to send.

Step 11. Read every word before you show me, and cut anything I can't act on. Show me one screen of short bullets: the funnel, my top random acts of AI, and what to do this week. Keep the full list with dates and proof ready, and show it when I ask.

A few things I need you to hold to:
- Every number is something you counted, and tell me what you counted it from. I'm going to say these numbers out loud in a room.
- "Never came up again" means you looked and found nothing. Don't say nobody read something, because you can't see who opened what.
- First names only. Leave out anything about clients, health, money or staff that I wouldn't want on a screen.
- If you can't see my history or a place I named, tell me what's missing next to your numbers, offer to take a pasted list, and keep going if I skip it. Don't guess.
- Write in plain words.

End with this share card for the room. Numbers only, nothing that names a person, client or project:

MY MIRROR · last 30 days
Made with AI: __
Went somewhere: __
Came up again: __
Moved something forward: __
My most common random act of AI: __ (a type, like "dashboard")
Prompt 2

The 5 secrets

One rule you paste into your Claude instructions so it asks before it builds anything.

What you get

  • Claude asks what problem you’re solving before it builds an app, SOP, dashboard or skill
  • A one-page plan you OK before anything gets built
  • A walk through every section before your team treats it as truth

About 2 minutesPaste it into your CLAUDE.md, project instructions or Cowork.

Before you build anything for me or my team (an app, dashboard, SOP, playbook, skill, artifact or new offer):
- Ask what problem we’re solving and whether it’s necessary. No reason, no build.
- Interview me and write a one-page plan. Wait for my OK.
- Ask where the truth for this lives. It can’t live in what you build.
- Show me how the best do it, give me a few versions, and build the simplest.
- Walk me through every section before it’s saved where my team treats it as truth.
Prompt 3

The 5 lines to tell Claude

One line for each secret. Say it when you start a build, or keep all 5 in your instructions.

What you get

  • One line each for Earn, Plan, Ground, Borrow and Perfect
  • Copy one line, or all 5 at once
  • Short enough to say to Claude at the start of any build

Under a minuteUse them every time you build.

  1. 1 · Earn

    Before you build anything, ask me what problem we’re solving and whether it’s necessary. If I don’t have a reason, don’t build it.

  2. 2 · Plan

    Interview me first. Write a one-page brief and wait for my OK before you build.

  3. 3 · Ground

    Ask me where the truth for this lives. It can’t live in the artifact. Flag anything that makes a second copy.

  4. 4 · Borrow

    Research how the best show this, give me 5 versions to react to, then build the simplest one a person can take in.

  5. 5 · Perfect

    Walk me through every section and make me justify it. Then ask where it gets saved: scratch, mine or the department. The department needs an approval.
