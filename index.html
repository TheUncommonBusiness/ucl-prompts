  1. Copy the Mirror below.
  2. Open Claude. Pick Sonnet 5.5 with thinking on high.

Prompt 1

The Mirror

Looks back at everything you made with AI in the last 30 days, measures it against where you want your business to go, and tells you what to do next.

What you get

  • Three moves to make this week, each with a first step you can take today
  • What to stop, by name, and the numbers behind it, measured against your 12-month goal
  • One rule to paste into Claude, plus a share card for the room

About 5 minuteson Sonnet 5.5, after three quick questions.

THE MIRROR

Audit the last 30 days of my work with AI against where I'm trying to take my business. Show me how much of what I made went anywhere, how much of it served my real goals, and what to do next. Keep it short and specific to me.

Start by zooming me out. Take a quick look at what you can see (our recent chats and projects, any instructions or notes you have for me, the titles of my recent meetings) so you can offer smart options. Keep that to a handful of searches. Then ask me these three questions one at a time, and wait for my answer before you ask the next one. Use the AskUserQuestion tool if you have it, or a short message if you don't. Let me pick more than one answer or write my own, and build each question's options from what you saw and what I've already told you.
1. Where do you want the business to be 12 months from now?
2. What's the biggest thing standing in the way of that?
3. What has to happen in the next 30 days to move it?
My answers are the yardstick for everything after this. Once I've answered the third question, don't ask me anything else until you're done.

Look only at what you can see quickly: our past chats, projects and artifacts, any files you can reach, and anything published. Work from names, dates and when things were last changed. Don't open documents, transcripts or code.

For proof that something went somewhere, use the quickest sign you have: it was changed or reused after the day it was made, it was shared or published, or it comes up by name in my team's chat or meeting notes. Keep it to about 15 searches, and spend them on the biggest items. If you search chat or email, use my team's workspace and skip any community Slack or personal inbox.

Judge every item against my three answers. It served my goal only if it moved the 12-month goal, worked on the thing in the way, or pushed the 30-day focus. Everything else was time spent somewhere else, however good it looks.

Write the result right here in the chat as one short screen of markdown: under about 250 words before the share card, no paragraphs, one line per point. Use this shape, in this order:

Open with one bold line, built from my own numbers and measured against my 12-month goal, that makes me stop and think. Make it true, and make it land. If the pile shows I'm working toward something else, that's the line.

Under it, one line with my answers: 12-month goal · In the way · Next 30 days.

### Do these 3 things this week
Three moves, numbered, each built on a real item, topic or goal from my answers and my pile. A move can be to finish something half-built that serves the goal, merge duplicates and say which one survives, repeat what made my wins work, start on a goal that got almost none of my time, or hand something off. At least one move has to work on the thing in the way. Each move gets exactly three lines: the move in bold, then "Why:" with one line of evidence (names, numbers, dates), then "First step (X min):" with one thing I can do today in under 15 minutes.

### Stop
Up to three things to stop, pause or archive, each by its real title with a count or dates. Group repeats under their topic, like "Brand style labs: 5 versions, Nov 3 to 9," so nothing hides in a count.

### The numbers
One small table: Made, Went somewhere, Served my goal, Made twice.

### Your rule
One line in quotes, drawn from my own pattern, ready to paste into my Claude instructions.

Then the share card below, filled in with numbers only and no notes inside it, and one short line on what you counted from. If I ask for more, show me the full list with dates and proof.

Hold to these:
- Be ruthless. Name what went nowhere, with dates, and don't soften it to spare my feelings.
- Every insight and every move names something real from my answers or my pile. Nothing that would fit anyone, like "focus on your priorities" or "use AI less."
- Every number is something you counted.
- "Went nowhere" only means you found no sign. You can't see who read what.
- If you can't see something, say what's missing in one line and keep going.

Instructions

The 5 secrets

One rule you paste into your Claude instructions so it asks before it builds anything.

What you get

  • Claude asks what pain it solves and which of your top goals it moves before it builds anything
  • A one-page plan you OK before anything gets built
  • A walk through every section before your team treats it as truth
  • A check before anything that will take more than an hour of your time

About 2 minutesPaste it into your CLAUDE.md, project instructions or Cowork.

Before you build anything for me or my team (an app, dashboard, SOP, playbook, skill, artifact or new offer):
- Ask what pain this is causing, what it costs my business, and which of my top goals fixing it would move. If it moves none, say so before we build.
- Interview me one question at a time and write a one-page plan. Open it with one line on the pain it solves, then the must-haves, what’s out of scope, and what done looks like. Wait for my OK.
- Ask where the truth for this lives. It can’t live in what you build.
- Show me how the best do it, give me a few versions, and build the simplest.
- Walk me through every section before it’s saved where my team treats it as truth.
If this will take more than an hour of my time, ask whether it’s the best use of that hour this week.
Prompt 2 · Secret 5, Perfect

Sushi Chef Polish Pass

Run it right after Claude makes anything for you. It checks every detail, fixes what it finds without touching your facts, and scores itself.

What you get

  • The same deliverable, polished page by page: type, spacing, color, images and numbers made consistent
  • Its three most memorable moments lifted, using only your brand and your content
  • A list of what it fixed, anything it flagged but didn’t change, and final scores out of 100

Nothing to fill inPaste it into the same chat, right after Claude makes your deliverable.

SUSHI CHEF POLISH PASS

ROLE: You are Paula Scher of Pentagram, working with Jessica Walsh of &Walsh and Steve Krug. Today you review with the eye of a Japanese sushi chef who has served sushi for 30 years and still checks every grain of rice before a plate leaves the kitchen.

CONTEXT: You just made a deliverable for me in this chat. It could be a proposal, deck, report, PDF, web page, document or any other output. Do not start over. Your job now is to make every element in it perfect and elevated.

STEP 1. INSPECT EVERY GRAIN.
Go page by page, slide by slide or section by section. List every element in each one: headings, body text, numbers, quotes, tables, charts, images, icons, buttons, links, footers and page numbers. Check each one for:
- Typography: clear hierarchy, consistent sizes, kerning, leading, no widows or orphans, comfortable line length.
- Spacing: same margins throughout, consistent padding, everything aligned to the grid, room to breathe.
- Color and contrast: only my brand colors (or one consistent palette if I don't have any), text easy to read on every background.
- Images and icons: sharp, never stretched or pixelated, one consistent style.
- Numbers: every number, price and total matches everywhere it appears, totals add up, same number format throughout.
- Words: no typos, every name spelled the same every time, no leftover placeholders or [brackets], no filler that sounds like AI.
- Consistency: the same kind of element looks the same everywhere.

STEP 2. FIX AS YOU GO.
Fix every problem you find. Never change a price, a number, a name, a quote or a claim. If one of those looks wrong, flag it for me instead.

STEP 3. ELEVATE THREE MOMENTS.
Find the three moments the reader will remember most, usually the opening, the most important idea or number, and the next step. Make each one feel like opening a package from Hermès, using only my brand and the content already in the deliverable.

STEP 4. RUN THREE QA PASSES.
1. Typography audit (kerning, leading, hierarchy)
2. Spacing review (margins, padding, visual breathing room)
3. Luxury perception check (Would LVMH publish this?)

STEP 5. SCORE YOURSELF.
Score the deliverable from 0 to 100 on typography, spacing, color, accuracy and overall luxury feel. If any score is under 90, fix what is holding it back and score again. Stop after two extra rounds.

STEP 6. HAND IT BACK.
Show me the polished deliverable. Under it, give me:
- What you fixed, section by section, one line each
- Anything you flagged but did not change, and why
- Your final scores
Quick lines

The 5 lines to tell Claude

One line for each secret. Say it when you start a build, or keep all 5 in your instructions.

What you get

  • One line each for Earn, Plan, Ground, Borrow and Perfect
  • Copy one line, or all 5 at once
  • Short enough to say to Claude at the start of any build

Under a minuteUse them every time you build.

  1. 1 · Earn

    Before you build anything, ask me what problem we’re solving and whether it’s necessary. If I don’t have a reason, don’t build it.

  2. 2 · Plan

    Interview me first. Write a one-page brief and wait for my OK before you build.

  3. 3 · Ground

    Ask me where the truth for this lives. It can’t live in the artifact. Flag anything that makes a second copy.

  4. 4 · Borrow

    Research how the best show this, give me 5 versions to react to, then build the simplest one a person can take in.

  5. 5 · Perfect

    Walk me through every section and make me justify it. Then ask where it gets saved: scratch, mine or the department. The department needs an approval.
